# How professional clippers turn long videos into short vertical clips

Research report for TikTok, YouTube Shorts and Instagram Reels, covering which moments to pick and how to edit them.
Date: 2026-11-27. Method: web research only, using 46 sources. The numbered list is at the end, and citations look like [S#].

**How to read this report**
- **(vendor)** marks a figure from a company that sells a clipping or editing tool. These are usually correlational studies with no published method, so treat them as directional.
- **(unverified)** marks a claim I could not confirm against a primary source.
- **DISAGREEMENT** flags places where sources conflict.
- Hooks are covered by a separate skill, so they appear here only where they affect selection or structure.

## 1. Moment and scene selection

### 1.1 Universal criteria (all genres)

1. **The clip must stand alone.** A stranger must understand it with zero context from the episode. Reject anything that needs a setup the viewer didn't see [S9][S8][S44].
2. **It must be useful or interesting, and true to the show's tone.** Don't pull a line out of context to make clickbait. Don't bend the show toward a micro-trend or meme [S9].
3. **Treat selection as elimination, not discovery.** In a typical 2-hour podcast, expect roughly 4–8 genuinely postable moments [S8].
4. **Four moment types that reliably work** [S8]:
   - A contradiction of what the audience believes.
   - A specific number or concrete fact.
   - An unguarded reaction: a pause, a laugh or a flinch.
   - A compressed how-to.
5. **Other proven moment types** [S10][S44][S43]:
   - A surprising statement from a guest.
   - Debate or tension, which drives comments.
   - Emotional peaks such as laughter, anger, tears or euphoria.
   - A sudden change of topic.
   - A story with a beginning, a middle and an end. One source calls this the rarest and most valuable type [S44].
   - A strong line that makes sense on its own.
6. **The setup-to-payoff gap is a hard limit.** Reject a moment if the payoff lands more than about 20 s after the setup [S8].
7. **The quality of the moment beats the quality of the edit.** "A technically perfect clip of a mediocre moment is still mediocre." Polished editing does not rescue a weak moment [S44].
8. **Signals that help you find candidates:**
   - **Audio:** volume peaks usually mean laughter [S9]. Faster speech and rising volume, or an off-script pause of about 2 s before an answer ("gold"), also mark a moment [S44].
   - **Video:** a change in facial expression, the speaker standing up or turning to the camera, or a change in gestures [S44].
   - **Agreement between signals:** when audio and video signals line up, the odds of a good clip go up [S44].
   - **Chat:** in streams, look for a flood of the same emote, all-caps bursts, or chat repeating the streamer's line [S44].
   - **Analytics:** audience-engagement heatmaps, such as StreamBee in Streamlabs, show where viewers were most engaged [S43].
9. **Cut by meaning, not by silence.** Start where the story starts, not where a silence ends. End where the arc closes, not where talking stops. Never cut into an emotional build just because there is a pause before the punchline [S44].

### 1.2 What to reject

- Intros, sponsor reads and inside jokes [S8].
- Clips that only make sense with context [S9].
- Routine chat in streams (greetings, "what's tomorrow's stream") [S44].
- Moments picked because they were easy to find, or funny with no arc [S44].
- In gaming: cutscenes, story reveals and endings, menus, loading screens and black screens [S11].
- Music-only footage. Without speech there are no hooks or captions, so these make weak clips [S11].
- A Shorts clip with copyrighted background music that runs over 60 s, which may be blocked [S11].

### 1.3 Scoring frameworks

- **OpusClip Virality Score** (0–99) [S1] (vendor):
  - It rates each candidate on Hook, Flow, Value and Trend.
  - Tiers: 80–100 means boost or prioritise; 50–79 means test organically; 0–49 means archive.
  - Opus says the score is a prediction and doesn't guarantee performance.
- **Manual equivalent** (my synthesis, not a published framework): score each candidate 1–5 on four questions.
  1. Does it stand alone?
  2. Does it have a clear payoff?
  3. Does it make the viewer feel something or teach something specific?
  4. Is it 45 s or less, or can it be cut to that?
  Publish the top scorers.
- **Editorial check before posting** [S44]:
  - Does it fit the channel's narrative?
  - Is the tone right for this week?
  - Will the reaction land with the target audience?

### 1.4 Clips per hour

**DISAGREEMENT.** Estimates vary by an order of magnitude, depending on the quality bar and on whether a tool or a human is choosing.

| Claim | Source |
|---|---|
| 4–8 truly postable clips per 2-hour episode | [S8] |
| About 6–10 per hour, or 20 "viral" clips from 2 h | [S10] (vendor) |
| 3–5 clips per game edited traditionally, versus 15–25 with AI | Opus sports figure via [S5] (vendor, unverified) |

- My recommendation: plan for about 3–5 strong clips per hour. Treat AI candidate lists as a long list for a human to cut down. One tool review found AI tools missed obvious highlights (unverified, search summary only).

### 1.5 Genre-specific rules

**Podcasts and interviews** [S8][S9][S10]
- Start on the answer, not the question. End one beat after the payoff.
- Cut on speaker changes, never mid-sentence.
- Target length is about 40 s. How-tos should run under 45 s. Go to 45–75 s only when the moment earns it.
- Follow the active speaker. For arguments and comedy, use a stacked split so both reactions show.

**Streams and gaming** [S11][S12][S13][S44]
- Favour reactions and action.
- Default length is 30–60 s. Cut.pro suggests 60–90 s for an ideal clip, about 90 s for stories and about 20 s for a single quote [S44].
- **DISAGREEMENT:** Clamper suggests 15–45 s for Reels [S12].
- Layout:
  - Put the facecam on top (about 25–30% of the frame) and gameplay below [S12].
  - Alternatively, use a 1080×640 facecam panel over a 1080×1280 gameplay panel [S13].
  - With no webcam, use a centre crop [S11].
  - A facecam scaled up 3–4× looks soft [S13].
- Keep the original game audio [S12].
- Clip other streamers only with permission. Publishers remove videos that are mostly story or ending footage [S11].

**Sports** [S14]: from Little Dot Studios' analysis of 1,430 sports Shorts.
- Clip extraordinary or unexpected action and funny moments that would never make a standard highlight package.
- Keep editing minimal.
- Sports moments go stale fast, so post quickly [S5].
- Results:
  - The best performers ran 51–60 s, and views tracked average view duration.
  - Shorts made natively for YouTube got about 18× more views than versions reformatted from TikTok or Reels.
  - Shorts did better *without* in-app text and music.
  - Views at 30 days were about 4× views on day 1.
  - Subscriber conversion was more than 5× that of long-form videos.

**Tutorials and educational content** [S4][S8]
- Use a compressed how-to: one problem, then one fix.
- Length: 25–40 s for tutorials and 35–45 s for educational clips [S4] (vendor). Under 45 s is also recommended [S8].
- For screen demos, fit the whole frame (blur-fill) rather than cropping away the information [S38].

**Comedy** [S4][S8]
- 18–28 s, with the payoff on a clean cut [S4] (vendor).
- Use a stacked split so the listener's reaction is visible [S8].

**Vlogs and storytelling** [S4]
- 30–45 s, with a new beat every 5–7 s (vendor).

**TV and movie scenes** [S26][S28][S6]
- Uncut scenes are the riskiest category. A clip with little narrative, or one others have uploaded many times, is "reused content" on YouTube [S28]. A copy with only borders, speed changes or credit is "unoriginal" on Instagram [S26].
- Fiction weighs against fair use [S6].
- What qualifies on YouTube: rewritten dialogue with a voiceover, critical commentary, or a storyline of your own [S28].
- Part 1/Part 2 series formats: no reliable source found (unverified).

**Music**
- No source-backed clipping framework was found (unverified).
- Copyright covers both the composition and the sound recording [S6].
- Library music on a platform is often licensed for personal use only [S6].

## 2. Clip structure

### 2.1 Arc

- **The structure is hook, context, build, payoff, then loop or end.** Galloway puts it this way: "Shorts are like intros that need a conclusion" [S18].
- **Put the payoff first when you can.** Show the outcome or the strongest frame up front, then rewind into how it happened [S5].
- **Move the strongest moment to where retention drops** [S5].
- **End while energy is high.** Don't let the clip fade out [S5].
- **Rearranging the order is allowed** as long as the meaning stays intact.
  - Cold-open on the answer or the reaction, then cut back to the question [S8][S5].
  - Never rearrange in a way that changes what a guest meant. Descript warns against out-of-context clickbait [S9].
- **In point:** the first word of the answer or story, not the question and not the silence before it [S8][S44].
- **Out point:** one beat after the payoff [S8]. For a loop, end on a line that flows into the opening line [S3][S20].

### 2.2 Platform length limits (2025–26)

| Platform | Maximum or rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| YouTube Shorts | Up to 3 min since 15 Oct 2024. YouTube's help page says that from 8 Dec 2025, square or vertical uploads of 3 min or less on standard channels register as Shorts. | [S29][S45] |
| Shorts with Content ID-claimed music | Clips over 1 min may be blocked | [S11] (repeated by many tool sources) |
| Instagram Reels | Reported at up to 3 min, up from 90 s (exact date unverified) | (unverified) |
| TikTok | Creator Rewards pays only for original videos **over 1 min** | [S32] |
| X Original Content Rewards (clipping campaigns) | Clip may be at most 30% of the source video | [S24] |

### 2.3 Recommended lengths

**DISAGREEMENT: this is the most contested topic.**

| Recommendation | Source | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 15–30 s sweet spot for Shorts. Under 10 s feels incomplete; over 45 s, drop-off gets sharp. | [S4] | vendor, no method given |
| 11–20 s band has the highest median Shorts views; recommends 11–30 s | [S17] | vendor, about 508k posts |
| Viral-tier TikToks have a median of 41 s (overall median 50 s, p90 179 s); 60–90 s is *under*-represented; checklist says 30–45 s | [S2] | vendor, 1,449 TikToks plus 34k clips |
| Most Shorts ran 20–40 s, but longer Shorts averaged more views. Shorts with an average view duration over 50 s averaged 4.1M views. | [S18] | Galloway, 5,400 Shorts, 2023 |
| Best sports Shorts ran 51–60 s | [S14] | 1,430 sports Shorts |
| About 40 s for podcasts | [S8] | |
| 60–90 s for an ideal stream clip | [S44] | vendor |
| 13 s or 60 s performed best | Nate Black via vidIQ (search summary only; unverified) | |

**Reconciled rule:** length should follow the moment.
- Default to 20–45 s.
- Go to 50–60 s only when the clip holds viewers. Galloway's point is to hold viewers rather than simply run longer [S18].
- If you're targeting TikTok Creator Rewards, the clip has to exceed 1 min [S32].
- Cutting the script by about 30% and speeding action up 1.2–1.5× are suggested ways to tighten [S4] (vendor). On YouTube, speed changes alone don't count as modification [S28].

## 3. Editing

### 3.1 Pacing and cut frequency

**DISAGREEMENT:** sources range from a cut every 1 s to a cut every 6 s.

| Cadence | Source |
|---|---|
| A cut every 2–4 s in high-performing Shorts | [S4] (vendor) |
| "Cut every few seconds"; vary shot lengths; cut on movement | [S3] (vendor) |
| Hormozi-style editing: cuts every 1–3 s | Choppity estimate (search summary; unverified) |
| Jump cuts every 3–4 s; auto-zoom every 6 s | tool blogs (search summary; unverified) |

- **Pattern interrupts** (zoom, angle change, graphic, SFX) must support the message, not just add noise. A widely quoted figure of "58% vs 32% retention" with interrupts has no published method, so treat it as unverified.
- **Punch-ins:** about 10% scale on a jump cut hides the cut and resets attention (unverified, single blog).
- **Opus usage data** (13.5M clips; vendor) [S3]:
  - Most top clips are edited *lightly*.
  - Text overlays appear in about 4.5% of clips, B-roll in 6.0%, music in under 2%, outros in 4.6% and branded intros in about 1%.
  - The sports study agrees: minimal editing [S14].

### 3.2 Silences, breaths and room tone

- **Trim pauses instead of deleting them.** Shorten them to about 0.3 s [S42]. Gaps under 0.3 s read as natural (unverified, Magica).
- **Tool defaults (DISAGREEMENT):**
  - Filmora and Audacity: 0.5 s at −20 dB.
  - Clipchamp: flags pauses over 3 s.
  - These come from search summaries, and the docs were not opened.
- **Breaths:** no sourced rule found (unverified). Common practice is to remove them in fast-paced clips and lower them in emotional ones.
- **Room tone:** record 20–30 s so cuts don't leave dead air [S3].
- **Don't remove meaningful silence.** A pause before a punchline or an answer is part of the moment [S44].

### 3.3 J-cuts, L-cuts and dialogue

- **J-cut:** the next shot's audio starts before its picture.
- **L-cut:** the previous shot's audio carries over the next picture [S46].
- In dialogue, a J-cut lets you stay on Person A's face as Person B starts talking. This avoids choppy ping-pong cutting [S46].
- In clips, use J/L cuts to move to a reaction shot, B-roll or a screen recording while the speaker keeps talking [S3][S46].

### 3.4 Reframing 16:9 to 9:16

| Situation | Method | Source |
|---|---|---|
| One speaker | Face-tracking crop. Keep the face in the upper ⅔ of the frame. | [S38][S8] |
| Two speakers, both must be visible (argument, comedy, interview reaction) | Stacked split-screen. Each half is only about 540 px tall versus 1,920 px for a full frame, so use it only when both faces matter. | [S38][S8] |
| Demos, slides, screen recordings, wide sports shots | Fit the whole frame with a blurred fill or letterbox. Some see this as "cheap" or "lazy", so prefer it only when every pixel matters. | [S38] |
| Gaming with facecam | Facecam on top (25–30%) with gameplay below | [S12][S13] |

- A static centre crop of a two-person table shot misses both faces [S38].
- "Letterbox" means different things in different tools, so check the preview.

### 3.5 Safe zones

**DISAGREEMENT:** none of these are official for organic posts. Platforms publish safe zones mainly for ads, so always preview in each app.

| Platform | Specification (1080×1920 canvas) | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Instagram Reels | Safe area about 1010×1280: top 220 px and bottom 420 px blocked. An alternative spec is 996×1400 (top 210, bottom 310). | [S16] / [S15] |
| TikTok | About 900×1492: top 108, bottom 320, right 120 px blocked | [S16] |
| YouTube Shorts | About 984×1500 with bottom 300 blocked. Other guides say 840×960 or 900×1160. | [S16] / others |
| Cross-platform | Centred 900×1400 box. Alternatively: top about 15% (288 px), sides 10–15%, bottom 25–35% (480–672 px). | [S16][S15] |
| Opus caption guidance | Keep out of the top 20% and bottom 25% | [S7] |

- UI covers about the bottom 15% at minimum [S3].
- Keep the right edge clear of the like, comment and share rail. Never let the last caption word touch the right edge [S15].

### 3.6 Captions

- **Use them.** Captions appear on 80.2% of top clips, and 78.6% of clips use animated captions [S3] (vendor).
  - Opus also says 85% of mobile viewers watch muted, and that burned-in captions are linked to 15–25% higher retention [S4]. Both claims are unsourced, so treat them as unverified.
- **Placement** [S15][S8][S7]:
  - Lower-middle or centre third, below the faces.
  - Don't cover the mouth or hands.
  - Never run two caption systems at once, such as auto-captions plus burned-in ones.
  - On TikTok, keep essential text out of the lowest quarter.
- **Chunking (DISAGREEMENT):** the two styles do different jobs. Use the kinetic style for clips.

| Style | Spec | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Kinetic or "Hormozi" clip style | 1–2 words per beat | [S3] |
| Kinetic or "Hormozi" clip style | 4–6 words over 2 lines, split at key words | CapCut guide (search summary) |
| Accessibility or subtitle style | 5–7 words, 32–42 characters per line, max 2 lines | [S7] |
| Premiere social preset | Max 15 characters | Premiere preset (search summary) |

- **Reading speed** [S39][S7]:
  - 12–17 characters per second is comfortable; Netflix caps at 20 CPS for adults.
  - Display each block for about 1.5–3 s, using 0.3 s per word plus 0.5 s of buffer.
  - Bring captions in 0.1–0.3 s before the audio, and keep them within 0.5 s of sync.
- **Style:**
  - Bold sans-serif fonts. Montserrat, Anton or TheBoldFont are typical.
  - High contrast with an outline, shadow or box. Use a black box at 60–80% opacity, or aim for at least a 4.5:1 contrast ratio [S7][S3].
  - Avoid all-caps sentences and keep caps for emphasis [S3]. **DISAGREEMENT:** the Hormozi style is all-caps [S36].
- **Hormozi preset** [S36]:
  - Old version: TheBoldFont, uppercase, with shadow.
  - New version: Anton, uppercase, slight shadow, yellow stroke and a pop-in animation.
  - Highlight keywords by recolouring or scaling them.
- **Size:** about 48–56 px at 1080 width (unverified, FluxNote). Opus's "18–24 pt" refers to YouTube's subtitle player, not burned-in text [S7].
- **Emojis:**
  - Animated emojis on keywords are a CapCut feature (search summary).
  - No source gives a frequency rule (unverified).
  - Use them sparingly, as with sound effects.

### 3.7 Audio: music, ducking, loudness and sound effects

- **Loudness (DISAGREEMENT):**
  - YouTube normalises to about −14 LUFS integrated with a −1 dBTP true peak. This is an *observed* figure; YouTube doesn't publish it [S37].
  - TikTok, Instagram and Facebook publish no target. TikTok is estimated at about −14. Meta uses adaptive xHE-AAC [S37].
  - Some guides say −10 to −12 LUFS for IG/TikTok (unverified).
  - Safe default: export at −14 LUFS and −1 dBTP.
- **Ducking music under speech:**
  - Duck by −6 to −12 dB, with 50–100 ms attack and 300–500 ms release (general guide; unverified).
  - Music sits about −15 dB under narration, −12 dB under casual dialogue and −6 dB in music-led moments (unverified).
  - Normalise loudness and duck music [S3].
- **Music use is rare in top clips:** under 2% use it [S3]. Sports Shorts did better *without* in-app music [S14]. Gaming clips do better with original audio [S12].
- **Sound effects:** whooshes and pops on cuts and zooms are common practice. No source gives a sourced dB level or frequency (unverified).

### 3.8 B-roll, emphasis and transitions

- **B-roll** appears in about 6% of top clips [S3]. Use it to illustrate a specific noun or number, carried by a J/L cut.
- **Emphasis:** punch in on the key line, recolour the keyword, or add a short text overlay. Don't overlay text that only describes what's already happening; on Instagram that doesn't count as an original edit [S26].
- **Transitions to avoid:**
  - Flashy, distracting wipes. Cut on movement instead [S3].
  - Branded intros and long outros. Only about 1% of top clips have a branded intro and 4.6% an outro [S3].
  - Fades at the end, which break loops [S20].

## 4. Retention

### 4.1 Benchmarks

**DISAGREEMENT:** metric definitions differ between sources.

| Metric | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Average completion | Shorts about 73%, TikTok about 78%, Reels about 65% | Socialinsider 2025 via [S41] |
| Shorts average view percentage | 73.6% over the 12 months to June 2026 | vidIQ (search summary) |
| Median view-through | About 45%; bottom 90% about 40%; top 10% about 91%. Read 40–45% as average and 50–60% as solid. | [S17] (vendor) |
| Shorts "viewed vs swiped away" | Under 60% rarely performs; best performers 70–90% | [S18] |
| Same metric, target | Aim for 75–80% | guides; YouTube publishes no official benchmark |
| 3-second hold | 70%+ still watching past 3 s; 50–60% of drop-off happens in the first 3 s | [S4] (vendor) |
| Completion for Shorts under 30 s | Over 60% | [S4] (vendor) |
| Over 100% average percentage viewed | Means rewatches (loops) | Shortimize / eMarketer (search summary) |

- Engagement (likes and comments) showed no strong correlation with Shorts views. Average view duration and viewed-vs-swiped did [S18].
- A YouTube Shorts view now counts on start or replay (since 31 Mar 2025). "Engaged views" are the stricter metric [S19].

### 4.2 Rewatches and loops

- Write the final sentence so it flows grammatically into the opening sentence [S20][S3].
- Join the audio seamlessly across the loop point, and add a still frame to cover the restart lag [S20].
- Looped Shorts that perform rarely run longer than about 7–8 s [S20].
- Loops don't guarantee double the watch time [S20].
- Opus says even a 10% replay rate helps [S4] (unsourced).

### 4.3 What kills retention

- Cold-start intros ("hey guys"), slow build-up, dead air and padding, and running too long [S17].
- A clip that needs context [S9]. A payoff that arrives more than 20 s after the setup [S8].
- Thumbnail or hook promises the clip doesn't keep. MrBeast's guide calls meeting the promise essential [S21][S22].
- Captions or faces hidden by the platform UI [S15][S16].
- Content reformatted from another platform: native Shorts got about 18× more views than reversioned ones in sports [S14].

## 5. Posting and packaging

### 5.1 Volume and cadence

- **Buffer study** (11.4M posts, 150k accounts, Oct 2025) [S33]:
  - Compared with posting once a week, views per post rose 17% at 2–5 posts a week, 29% at 6–10 and 34% at 11+.
  - The *median* post stays around 500 views at every frequency. The upside comes from the tail: p90 rose from about 3.7k to about 14.4k.
- **For podcasts:** post 2–3 clips in the 48 h after an episode drops, spread the rest over the week, and hold the strongest clip for mid-week [S8].
- **Clip accounts** reportedly post 5–8 a day on TikTok, 4–6 on Reels and 6–10 on Shorts (vendor estimate; unverified).
- Channels with 200+ Shorts saw views rise steadily [S19].
- **Shorts compound over time:** views at 30 days were about 4× views on day 1 in sports [S14]. Review each video at 7 days [S18].

### 5.2 Titles, on-screen text, covers and hashtags

- **YouTube:** titles and descriptions matter for Shorts search, and #Shorts is unnecessary [S19].
- **Cover frames:**
  - TikTok: pick a frame. Cover text appears on the profile grid, not in the feed (search summary).
  - YouTube Shorts thumbnails: **DISAGREEMENT.** Google's help page describes selecting a frame from the video [S30]. vidIQ, Uppbeat and others say custom Shorts thumbnails rolled out in 2025 (unverified). Check in YouTube Studio.
- **On-screen text:**
  - Use a short title-card line in the top safe zone. Third-person storylines appear in 77.8% of top clips, and the "Expert Explainer" storyline is the most frequent [S2] (vendor).
  - In sports, Shorts did better without in-app text [S14].
- **Hashtags on TikTok** [S2] (vendor, correlation only):
  - Viral-tier clips averaged 10.4 hashtags versus 4.8 in the bottom tier.
  - A mixed strategy (niche plus generic) got about 2.5× the median views of niche-only (527.5k vs 210k).
  - Suggested mix: 8–12 tags in total, 5–8 niche and 2–4 generic.
  - Posts with #fyp had a higher median (485.6k vs 221.9k), but that is correlation only.

### 5.3 A/B testing

- **Instagram Trial Reels** (Meta, Dec 2024) [S34]:
  - Shown only to non-followers. Insights arrive after 24 h.
  - Optionally shared to followers after 72 h if the Reel performs.
  - Requires a public professional account. The 1,000+ follower threshold comes from secondary reports (unverified).
  - Trial Reels can now be scheduled (Social Media Today, search summary).
- Judge Instagram tests on watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach. Mosseri calls these the main ranking signals, with sends mattering most for reaching non-followers [S35]. The often-quoted "a send weighs 3–5× a like" is unverified.
- Test three different openings of the same clip on different days [S5] (vendor).

### 5.4 Platform differences

- **YouTube Shorts** [S19][S18][S14]:
  - Distribution starts with a seed audience, then expands or stops (explore/exploit).
  - Viewed-vs-swiped and average view duration drive it.
  - Titles are searchable, and views keep growing for weeks.
- **TikTok** [S31][S32][S2]:
  - The For You feed excludes unoriginal content.
  - Clips over 1 min are needed for Creator Rewards.
  - Hashtags still correlate with reach.
- **Instagram Reels** [S35][S26][S27]:
  - Ranking runs on watch time plus likes and sends per reach.
  - Originality enforcement is the strictest of the three platforms.
  - Trial Reels are available for testing.

### 5.5 Originality, copyright and fair use

**Instagram** [S26][S27][S40]
- **Edits that do NOT make a post original:**
  - Borders or frames, watermarks, speed changes.
  - Credit alone, or a new caption.
  - Overlaid text that only describes the video.
- **Edits that DO make a post original:**
  - Text that does more than describe, or narration.
  - A storyline, or graphics that recontextualise.
  - Adding your own perspective or a joke.
- **The test:** if your contribution could be removed and the content stays essentially the same, it needs more work [S26].
- **What happens to copies:**
  - When identical copies exist, only the original is recommended, and copies get a label linking to it.
  - Aggregator accounts are ineligible for recommendations.
  - Meme edits, voiceovers and reaction remixes are not replaced by the original [S27].
- **Getting eligibility back:** post a significant share of original content within a rolling 30-day window [S26].
- The rules extended to photos and carousels in 2026 [S40].

**YouTube** [S28]
- **ALLOWED:**
  - Critical reviews.
  - A movie scene with rewritten dialogue and a voiceover.
  - Sports replays that explain how a competitor succeeded.
  - Reactions with commentary.
  - Your own storyline.
  - Substantial audio and visual editing.
- **NOT allowed:**
  - Clips with little narrative.
  - Compilations from other social sites.
  - Content others have uploaded many times.
  - Content with no substantive modification.
  - Reactions that are mostly non-verbal.
  - Reading others' material aloud.
  - Pitch or speed changes alone.
- The policy is applied **channel-wide**, and having permission does not exempt you.
- On 15 Jul 2025, "repetitious content" was renamed "inauthentic content".
- A reported 2025 Shorts update reduces reach for re-uploaded Shorts (unverified).

**TikTok** [S31]
- Unoriginal content with no new or creative changes is ineligible for the For You feed.

**Fair use** [S6]
- Fair use is a *defense*, not a right.
- There is no safe number of seconds, credit is not permission, and disclaimers carry no weight.
- Fiction weighs against fair use, while news footage is safer.
- Commentary must be substantive.
- Song copyright has two parts: the composition and the recording.
- Get guest releases that cover short-form repurposing.
- Disclose sponsorships under FTC rules.

### 5.6 Clipping-program rules (Content Rewards, Whop)

- **Why clips get rejected** [S23]:
  - Submitted more than 30 min after posting.
  - Submitted to more than one campaign per post.
  - Wrong hook, tag or length for the brief.
  - Missing FTC disclosure.
  - Collab feature used without permission.
  - Reused edits.
  - Missing analytics proof.
- **Penalties and appeals** [S23]:
  - Fake views lead to a permanent ban.
  - One appeal per clip, decided in 2–7 days.
- **Pay** [S24]:
  - Most campaigns pay about $1 per 1,000 views. November 2026 median rates were YouTube $1.10 and others $1.00.
  - Streamer campaigns pay $0.25–$3 per 1,000 views in 10-day rounds.
  - The typical cap is $200 per clip, with brand caps up to $750.
  - Brands have a 7-day review window. There is no follower minimum.
- **How clips are judged:** the campaign's "Content requirements" box is the only basis [S25].
- Content Rewards has split from Whop [S24].

## Quick checklist (synthesis)

1. Shortlist moments by elimination. Each must stand alone, have a payoff within 20 s of the setup, and fit one of the moment types in section 1.1.
2. Start on the answer, end one beat after the payoff, and cut on speaker changes. Move the strongest frame to the front.
3. Default length is 20–45 s. Go to 50–60 s only if the clip holds; on TikTok, go over 60 s for Creator Rewards.
4. Reframe: face-track one speaker, stack-split for debates, blur-fill for demos. Keep faces in the upper ⅔.
5. Trim silences to about 0.3 s, keep dramatic pauses, and use J/L cuts for reactions and B-roll.
6. Use kinetic captions of 1–3 words, placed lower-middle inside the safe zone, high contrast, with keyword highlights.
7. Export at −14 LUFS and −1 dBTP. Duck music by 6–12 dB under speech, or use no music.
8. End on a line that loops into the opening. No fade-outs, no branded intros.
9. Add a real transformation (commentary, narration, storyline) wherever the footage isn't yours.
10. Post 2–3 clips in the first 48 h, then keep a steady cadence. Review at 7 days. Test openings with Trial Reels.
